Once you've decided to hire a virtual assistant, the next decision is *how*. Broadly, you can hire an independent freelancer, often through a marketplace, or work with a virtual assistant agency that provides a managed service.

Both approaches can work well. This guide compares them so you can choose the right fit for your business.

## What's the difference?

**A freelancer** is an independent professional you contract directly. You find them, vet them, agree terms and manage the relationship yourself.

**An agency** is a business that employs or contracts assistants and provides them to clients as a service. The agency usually helps scope the role, matches you with an assistant, supports onboarding and remains involved in quality and continuity.

## Comparing the two

### Finding and vetting

With freelancers, you're the recruiter. You'll write the job post, review applications, interview candidates and run test tasks. Marketplaces show reviews and work history, which help, but the final judgment is yours.

With an agency, much of that work is done for you. A good agency assesses communication, reliability and skills before matching you. You should still meet your assistant, but you're choosing from a shortlist rather than starting from scratch.

### Cost structure

Freelancers set their own rates and you pay for their time directly. Agency pricing typically includes more than the assistant's hours: recruitment, matching, account management, quality oversight and sometimes backup cover.

That means an agency's rate may look higher than a freelancer's on paper. The real question is total cost, including your time spent recruiting and managing. We break this down in [How Much Does a Virtual Assistant Cost?](/blog/how-much-does-a-virtual-assistant-cost/).

### Continuity

This is one of the biggest differences. If a freelancer becomes unavailable, they take a full-time job, get sick or move on, you start again. With an agency, there's usually a plan for continuity, and documented processes make handovers easier.

### Quality control

Freelancers are responsible for their own quality, and you're responsible for monitoring it. Agencies typically have their own standards, check-ins and escalation routes. If something isn't working, you have someone to talk to besides the assistant.

### Flexibility

Freelancers can be very flexible for one-off or project work. Agencies tend to be well suited to ongoing support and can often add hours or specialist skills as you grow.

### Accountability and contracts

With a freelancer, your agreement is with an individual. With an agency, it's with a business, which typically means clearer service terms, invoicing and confidentiality agreements. If you hire individuals directly, make sure you understand worker classification rules where you operate.

## When a freelancer is a great choice

- You need a specific, one-off project done.
- You have time and confidence to recruit and manage.
- You've found someone with exactly the niche skill you need.
- Your budget is tight and the work is low-risk.

## When an agency is a great choice

- You want ongoing support and value continuity.
- You don't have time to recruit or manage closely.
- You want help scoping the role and setting up processes.
- The work involves your customers, confidential information or important systems.
- You may want to scale support up or add specialist skills later.

## Questions to ask either option

Whichever route you choose, ask:

1. How do you handle confidentiality and data security?
2. What tools are you familiar with?
3. What hours will you work, and how much overlap will we have?
4. How do you report on work completed?
5. What happens if you're unavailable?
6. Can we start with a trial period or defined scope?

## Red flags

Be cautious of:

- Guarantees that sound too good to be true ("double your revenue")
- Reviews or testimonials that can't be verified
- Reluctance to put terms in writing
- Requests for full owner access to your accounts on day one
- Vague answers about who will actually do the work

## A quick decision guide

Still unsure? Answer these four questions:

1. **Is the work ongoing or one-off?** Ongoing support leans toward an agency; one-off projects lean toward freelancers.
2. **How much time can you spend recruiting?** Little time leans toward an agency.
3. **How painful would it be if your assistant disappeared next month?** Very painful leans toward an agency with continuity arrangements.
4. **Do you need a rare, specialist skill for a short period?** A carefully chosen freelancer may be ideal.

There's no wrong answer, some businesses use an agency for core support and occasional freelancers for specialist projects.

## Where to go from here

Freelancers offer choice and direct control, making them great for defined projects. Agencies offer structure, continuity and shared responsibility, making them a strong choice for ongoing support. Think about how much time you want to spend recruiting and managing, and how important continuity is to your business.
