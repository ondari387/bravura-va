"How much does a virtual assistant cost?" is one of the first questions business owners ask, and one of the hardest to answer with a single number. Prices vary widely, and published rates rarely tell the whole story.

Rather than quote figures that will be out of date or don't apply to your situation, this guide explains **what drives the cost** of virtual assistance, the hidden costs most people forget, and how to compare options fairly as you plan for 2027.

## Why VA pricing varies so much

Two businesses can both say they "hired a VA" and be paying very different amounts for very different things. That's because the term covers everything from a part-time admin helper to a senior executive assistant managing a CEO's entire schedule.

The main factors are below.

### 1. Hours and commitment

The most obvious driver is how much support you need. Common arrangements include:

- **Ad hoc or project-based:** you pay for specific tasks or a defined project.
- **Part-time:** a set number of hours per week or month.
- **Dedicated or full-time:** an assistant focused on your business for a full working week.

Per-hour rates are sometimes lower for larger, longer commitments, because they're more predictable for the provider.

### 2. Skills and complexity

General administrative support is priced differently from specialist work. Tasks that require specific expertise (bookkeeping administration, design, website updates, e-commerce platform management or executive-level support) typically cost more than routine data entry.

Complexity matters too. Work that involves judgment, client contact or multiple systems requires a more experienced assistant than highly repetitive tasks.

### 3. Location

Where an assistant is based affects cost because local wages and living costs differ around the world. US-based VAs generally charge more than assistants based in countries such as Kenya, the Philippines or India.

But location isn't just about price. Consider time-zone overlap, communication style and the support structure around the assistant. We compare destinations in [Kenya vs Philippines vs India](/blog/kenya-vs-philippines-vs-india-virtual-assistant-outsourcing/).

### 4. Hiring model

How you hire changes both the price and what's included:

- **Freelancers** set their own rates. You handle recruitment, management and continuity.
- **Managed agencies** include matching, onboarding support, quality oversight and often backup cover in their pricing.
- **Employees** cost a salary plus benefits, taxes, equipment and management time.

Comparing a freelancer's hourly rate with an agency's rate is rarely like-for-like, because the agency is providing more than the hours.

### 5. Working-hour requirements

If you need an assistant available during specific hours (say, live phone coverage throughout the US business day from overseas), that can affect pricing. Flexible, asynchronous arrangements are usually simpler to staff.

### 6. Engagement length

Short trials and one-off projects may be priced differently from long-term arrangements.

## The hidden costs people forget

When comparing options, look beyond the headline rate.

**Your management time.** An inexpensive assistant who needs constant correction can cost you more in lost hours than a more experienced one.

**Recruitment time.** Posting jobs, reviewing applications, interviewing and testing candidates all take time, and you may have to repeat it if the first hire doesn't work out.

**Turnover.** If an assistant leaves, you lose their knowledge of your business. Documented processes and a provider who can help with continuity reduce that risk.

**Tools and subscriptions.** Your assistant may need seats in your CRM, helpdesk or project tools.

**Mistakes.** Errors in invoices, customer replies or data entry have real costs. Accuracy is worth paying for.

## How to calculate whether a VA is worth it

Rather than asking "what's the cheapest option?", ask "what's this support worth to my business?"

A simple approach:

1. **List the tasks you'd delegate** and estimate the hours you spend on them each week.
2. **Estimate the value of your time.** What could you do with those hours: sell, serve clients, develop products?
3. **Consider the cost of things not getting done:** slow customer replies, missed follow-ups, unsent invoices.
4. **Compare that value** with the cost of support.

For many business owners, the return comes less from the hourly saving and more from what they can do with the time they get back.

## How to compare quotes fairly

When you receive quotes, ask:

- What exactly is included: just hours, or matching, onboarding and oversight too?
- How are hours tracked and reported?
- What happens if the assistant is sick or leaves?
- Are there setup fees, minimum terms or notice periods?
- Which currency will you be billed in, and how are payments made?
- How are confidentiality and data security handled?

A reputable provider will answer clearly and put everything in writing.

## Should you start small?

Usually, yes. Starting with a part-time arrangement focused on a few clearly defined tasks lets you test delegation without a large commitment. Once you see the results, and identify more work to hand over, you can increase hours with confidence.

## Questions to ask yourself before requesting quotes

You'll get more accurate, comparable quotes if you know the answers to these first:

- Roughly how many hours per week of support do you need now, and in six months?
- Which tasks are most important, and which require specialist skills?
- How much live overlap with your working day do you need?
- Which tools will your assistant need to use?
- Who will be the assistant's main point of contact in your business?
- How will you measure whether support is working?

Sharing this information up front helps any provider give you a realistic picture rather than a generic rate.

## Final thoughts

The cost of a virtual assistant depends on hours, skills, location, hiring model and working arrangements. Focus on total value (including your time, quality and continuity) rather than the lowest hourly rate. The right support should pay for itself in time, focus and opportunities you can finally pursue.
