**A virtual assistant (VA) is a professional who handles business tasks for you remotely.** They work from their own location using the same cloud tools your team uses, such as email, shared drives, your CRM, helpdesk or online store, and take ownership of recurring work so you can focus on customers, decisions and growth.

That's the short answer. The more useful answer depends on the kind of help your business needs, so this guide walks through the main types of work, what a VA typically doesn't do, and how to work out what to delegate.

## The main types of work a virtual assistant handles

### Administrative and executive support

This is where most businesses start. A VA can:

- Triage your inbox, file messages and draft routine replies
- Manage your calendar, book meetings and coordinate across time zones
- Plan travel and build itineraries
- Prepare documents, presentations and reports
- Take meeting notes and chase action points
- Keep your CRM and spreadsheets up to date

For founders and executives, an [executive virtual assistant](/services/executive-virtual-assistant/) takes this further, with meeting preparation, stakeholder correspondence and closer support for one person.

### Customer support

A VA can answer customer emails and live chat using your knowledge base, handle order and booking enquiries, process returns within your policy and escalate anything that needs a decision. See our [customer support service](/services/customer-support/) for how this is usually set up.

### E-commerce and Shopify admin

For online stores, a VA can upload products, write and update descriptions, keep inventory data accurate, check orders, answer "where is my order?" questions and coordinate returns. Our [Shopify virtual assistant](/services/ecommerce-virtual-assistant/) page includes example workflows.

### Sales and lead generation support

A VA can research prospects, build and verify lead lists, enrich CRM records, log activity after calls and book meetings for your sales team. They support the sales process; closing deals stays with you. See [lead generation](/services/lead-generation/).

### Marketing and website support

Scheduling social posts, formatting newsletters, publishing blog posts, updating WordPress pages and producing simple graphics from templates are all common VA tasks. See [social media support](/services/social-media-management/) and [WordPress support](/services/wordpress-virtual-assistant/).

### Industry-specific admin

Many VAs specialise. Real estate teams delegate listing admin and viewing coordination. Recruitment agencies delegate interview scheduling and ATS updates. Small businesses delegate invoicing and receipt capture for their bookkeeper.

## What a virtual assistant usually doesn't do

Clear boundaries make delegation work. A VA typically does **not**:

- Make strategic decisions that belong to you
- Provide regulated professional services, such as legal, medical, tax or financial advice
- Carry out licensed activities, such as negotiating property deals
- Work in person at your premises (they're remote)
- Replace a specialist where deep expertise is needed, such as custom software development

A good VA provider will tell you when a task needs someone else.

## How working with a virtual assistant works day to day

Most VA relationships follow a simple rhythm:

1. **You agree what to delegate.** A short list of recurring tasks is the best start.
2. **You give access securely.** Individual logins and a password manager, not shared passwords.
3. **You explain each task once.** A quick screen recording is often enough; your VA turns it into a written checklist.
4. **Your VA works in your tools** during agreed hours, often with some overlap with your day.
5. **You get regular updates.** An end-of-day summary, a shared task board or a weekly check-in.

If your assistant is in another time zone, work can happen while you sleep. For example, Nairobi is 7-8 hours ahead of US Eastern time and 2-3 hours ahead of the UK. ## Virtual assistant vs AI assistant

AI tools can draft emails, summarise meetings and answer simple questions, and good VAs use them where it helps. The difference is responsibility: a human assistant checks the output, handles exceptions, notices when something looks wrong and follows through until the job is done. Read [AI Virtual Assistant vs Human Virtual Assistant](/blog/ai-virtual-assistant-vs-human-virtual-assistant/) for a fuller comparison.

## How to decide what to delegate

Try this for one week: note every task that takes more than ten minutes. Then mark each one:

- **Only I can do this** (strategy, key relationships, final decisions)
- **Someone else could do this with guidance**
- **This doesn't need doing at all**

The middle group is your starting job description. Good first candidates are tasks that are frequent, time-consuming and follow a pattern, such as inbox triage, scheduling, customer emails and invoicing. ## In summary

A virtual assistant takes recurring, process-driven work off your plate, from inbox and calendar to customers, sales admin and store operations. The best results come from starting with a clear list, explaining tasks well and agreeing how you'll communicate.
