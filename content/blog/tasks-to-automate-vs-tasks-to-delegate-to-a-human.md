Business owners today have more ways than ever to get work off their plate. Some tasks can be fully automated. Others are best handled by a human assistant. Many work best with a combination.

Choosing well saves money, reduces errors and keeps customers happy. Here's a practical framework.

## The four-question framework

For any task, ask:

### 1. Is it predictable?

If the task follows the same steps every time with the same inputs, it's a candidate for automation. If it varies, different situations, unusual inputs, a human is better.

### 2. Does it involve a relationship?

Tasks involving customers, partners, candidates or colleagues benefit from human judgment and tone, even if tools help.

### 3. What happens if it goes wrong?

Low-risk tasks can be automated with light monitoring. High-risk tasks (money, legal commitments, upset customers) need human oversight.

### 4. How often does it happen?

Automation pays off for high-volume tasks. A task done twice a year usually isn't worth automating.

## Tasks that are usually best automated

- **Moving data between systems:** form submissions to a CRM, orders to a spreadsheet
- **Notifications and reminders:** appointment reminders, payment due notices
- **Recurring invoices** for fixed amounts
- **Standard confirmations:** order received, booking confirmed
- **Backups and file syncing**
- **Simple routing rules:** labelling emails, assigning tickets by category

These are predictable, repetitive and low-judgment.

## Tasks that are usually best delegated to a human

- **Customer complaints and sensitive conversations**
- **Inbox triage:** deciding what's urgent and how to respond
- **Calendar management with competing priorities**
- **Lead qualification** that requires reading between the lines
- **Supplier and vendor communication**
- **Research requiring source evaluation**
- **Quality checks** on data, content and automations
- **Process documentation** and improvement

These require context, judgment, empathy or accountability.

## Tasks that work best combined

Many tasks benefit from both:

| Task | Automation / AI does | Human does |
|---|---|---|
| Customer email | Suggests a reply, pulls order data | Checks, personalizes, resolves |
| Meeting notes | Transcribes and summarizes | Confirms decisions, assigns and follows up actions |
| Social media | Schedules posts, suggests captions | Writes in brand voice, replies to comments |
| Data entry | Extracts data from documents | Verifies accuracy, handles exceptions |
| Reporting | Pulls numbers into a template | Explains changes, flags concerns |
| Lead management | Captures leads and sends first confirmation | Qualifies, follows up personally |

## Common mistakes

**Automating a broken process.** If the process doesn't work manually, automation makes it fail faster. Fix it first.

**Automating relationships.** Fully automated responses to unhappy customers often make things worse.

**Forgetting maintenance.** Automations break when tools change. Someone needs to monitor them.

**Over-delegating mechanical work.** Paying a person to copy and paste data that could flow automatically isn't a good use of their skills.

**Under-delegating judgment.** Expecting a chatbot to handle exceptions it was never designed for.

## A simple exercise

1. List every recurring task in your business for a week.
2. For each one, answer the four questions above.
3. Sort tasks into three columns: **Automate**, **Delegate**, **Combine**.
4. Start with the tasks that take the most time.

You'll often find that a human assistant is the right person to set up and monitor your automations, too.

## The role of a modern virtual assistant

The best virtual assistants today don't just do tasks, they help businesses decide what should be automated, set up simple automations, monitor them and handle everything that needs a person. That's far more valuable than either automation or manual work alone.

## Putting it together

Automate what's predictable, repetitive and low-risk. Delegate what involves relationships, judgment and accountability. Combine the two for everything in between. The result is a business that runs efficiently without losing the human touch.
