**Canadian businesses can hire a virtual assistant locally or remotely from another country, and a remote assistant often makes sense for recurring admin, customer support, e-commerce and real estate tasks.** The main things to plan are time-zone overlap, privacy and anti-spam rules, currency and how you'll hand work over.

This guide covers each of those, so you can hire with confidence.

## What Canadian businesses commonly delegate

- **Admin and executive support:** inbox, calendar, travel, documents and follow-ups
- **Customer support:** email and chat enquiries, order questions and returns
- **E-commerce:** Shopify product uploads, inventory updates and order admin
- **Real estate admin:** enquiry responses, showing coordination and CRM updates
- **Bookkeeping groundwork:** invoicing, receipt capture and payment reminders in QuickBooks or Xero
- **Marketing support:** social scheduling, newsletters and blog publishing

See [What Does a Virtual Assistant Do?](/blog/what-does-a-virtual-assistant-do/) for more detail on each.

## Working across Canadian time zones

Canada spans six time zones, so the right arrangement depends on where you are. If your assistant is based in Nairobi, Kenya (UTC+3, no daylight saving):

| Your time zone | Example cities | Nairobi is... |
|---|---|---|
| Newfoundland | St. John's | 5.5-6.5 hours ahead |
| Atlantic | Halifax | 6-7 hours ahead |
| Eastern | Toronto, Ottawa, Montreal | 7-8 hours ahead |
| Central | Winnipeg | 8-9 hours ahead |
| Mountain | Calgary, Edmonton | 9-10 hours ahead |
| Pacific | Vancouver | 10-11 hours ahead |

The smaller number applies during daylight saving time. Most of Saskatchewan stays on Central Standard Time all year.

**In practice:**

- **Eastern and Atlantic businesses** get live overlap during their morning.
- **Western businesses** usually combine a short live window with work completed overnight, ready for the morning.
- Agree working hours and response expectations in writing before you start.

## Privacy: PIPEDA and provincial laws

The Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) applies to personal information handled in commercial activity in most of Canada. Quebec, Alberta and British Columbia have their own private-sector privacy laws, and Quebec's Law 25 adds specific requirements, including around transfers outside the province.

If your assistant will handle customer or employee information:

- Limit access to what each task needs
- Use individual logins and a password manager
- Put confidentiality and data handling in writing
- Check that your privacy notice covers service providers outside Canada

The [Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada](https://www.priv.gc.ca/) publishes guidance for businesses. This isn't legal advice, so confirm your obligations with an adviser.

## Anti-spam: CASL

Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) has strict consent rules for commercial electronic messages. If your assistant helps with email outreach or newsletters, make sure your lists have valid consent, messages identify your business and every message includes a working unsubscribe option.

## Currency and pricing

Ask for quotes in Canadian dollars so you aren't exposed to surprise conversions. Compare what's included, not just the rate: matching, onboarding, quality checks and cover all affect value. See [Hourly vs Monthly Virtual Assistant Services](/blog/hourly-vs-monthly-virtual-assistant-services/).

## A simple onboarding plan

1. **Week 1:** kickoff call, tool access and your first two or three tasks
2. **Week 2:** your assistant works with your review and feedback
3. **Week 3:** lighter review; add new tasks
4. **Week 4:** a short review of what's working and what to delegate next

## In summary

A remote virtual assistant can give Canadian businesses reliable help with the work that eats into evenings. Plan overlap around your province's time zone, respect PIPEDA and CASL, get quotes in CAD and start with a clear list of tasks.
