Hiring a virtual assistant in Kenya can give US businesses a big advantage: work keeps moving while you sleep. But it only works smoothly with the right routines.

This guide covers practical ways to manage a Kenya-based assistant from the United States, from planning around time zones to building trust.

## Understand the time difference

Kenya uses East Africa Time (UTC+3) all year. Because the US observes daylight saving and Kenya doesn't, the gap changes twice a year:

| US time zone | Difference during daylight time | Difference during standard time |
|---|---|---|
| Eastern | Kenya is 7 hours ahead | Kenya is 8 hours ahead |
| Central | 8 hours ahead | 9 hours ahead |
| Mountain | 9 hours ahead | 10 hours ahead |
| Arizona (most of state) | 10 hours ahead | 10 hours ahead |
| Pacific | 10 hours ahead | 11 hours ahead |

**Tip:** Put both time zones in your calendar app so you can see your assistant's local time at a glance.

## Choose your working model

There are three common approaches.

### 1. Overlap-focused

Your assistant works a Nairobi afternoon-and-evening schedule that overlaps with your morning. Best for East Coast and Central clients who want real-time collaboration for part of the day.

### 2. Overnight delivery

Your assistant works Nairobi daytime hours, your overnight, and delivers completed work before you wake up. Best for asynchronous tasks like inbox triage, research, data entry and reporting.

### 3. Hybrid

A short overlap window for live questions, plus overnight work for everything else. This is the most popular option for many US businesses.

Agree the model, exact hours and expected response times before your assistant starts.

## Set up an end-of-day handover

The most important routine in a cross-time-zone relationship is the handover. At the end of your day:

1. Add new tasks to your shared task board with clear instructions.
2. Flag priorities for the next morning.
3. Leave context (links, files, short Loom videos) so your assistant isn't blocked.

At the end of their day, your assistant sends a summary:

- What was completed
- What's in progress
- Questions or decisions needed from you
- Anything urgent

You start your morning with a clear picture instead of a pile of unknowns.

## Communicate asynchronously, well

Async communication is a skill. A few principles help:

**Be complete.** Write requests that can be actioned without a follow-up question. Include the goal, the deadline, the relevant files and what "done" looks like.

**Use video for complex tasks.** A two-minute screen recording often replaces a page of instructions.

**Batch questions.** Encourage your assistant to collect non-urgent questions into one message rather than sending them throughout the day.

**Agree on urgency labels.** Decide what counts as urgent and how it should be flagged (for example, a specific Slack channel or a phone message).

## Use the overlap window wisely

If you have an overlap window, use it for:

- Quick decisions and clarifications
- Live calls with customers or partners your assistant supports
- Short weekly check-ins
- Reviewing work that benefits from discussion

Avoid filling the overlap with status updates that could be written.

## Track work transparently

Use a shared tool that suits your style: Asana, Trello, ClickUp, Notion or even a well-organized shared document. The goal is visibility without micromanagement.

Many US clients also like a short weekly report: what was done, what improved, what's next.

## Plan for US holidays and Kenyan holidays

Kenya has its own public holidays, and the US has its own. Share a calendar of both early in the relationship and plan coverage around them.

## Keep access secure

- Give your assistant individual accounts rather than shared logins
- Use a password manager for any shared credentials
- Turn on two-factor authentication
- Grant only the access each task needs
- Review access regularly

## Build the relationship

Remote doesn't have to mean distant. Strong working relationships are built on:

- **Context.** Share your goals, customers and why tasks matter.
- **Recognition.** Acknowledge good work: it matters wherever people are.
- **Feedback.** Give it promptly and specifically.
- **Inclusion.** Invite your assistant to relevant team meetings and channels.

## A sample day for an East Coast business

In this example, the assistant works 11 a.m.-8 p.m. Nairobi time, 4 a.m.-1 p.m. Eastern during daylight time.

- **8:00 a.m. ET (3:00 p.m. Nairobi):** A summary of the work done since 4 a.m. is waiting. Inbox is triaged.
- **8:30-10:30 a.m. ET:** Overlap window. Quick questions answered; a customer call is scheduled.
- **1:00 p.m. ET:** Your assistant's day ends with an end-of-day summary; you work as normal.
- **5:30 p.m. ET:** You add tomorrow's tasks and priorities to the board.
- **Early morning ET (before you wake):** Your assistant starts the next day: completing research, CRM updates and reporting from your handover list.

## Where to go from here

Managing a Kenya-based assistant from the US works best when you embrace the time difference rather than fight it. Choose a working model, build a strong daily handover, communicate clearly and asynchronously, and invest in the relationship. Do that, and you'll find your business keeps moving around the clock.
